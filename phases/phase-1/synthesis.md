# Synthesis — Phase I

## From Historical Comparison to Conceptual Framework

Plato and Aristotle are often interpreted as offering competing theories of mind. However, the analysis developed in this phase suggests a different interpretation.

Their difference is not merely doctrinal, but structural.

They do not simply give different answers to the same question.  
They reorganize what counts as the question itself.

Plato prioritizes the problem of stable knowledge.  
Aristotle prioritizes the organization of living activity.

This shift represents a transformation in explanatory priorities rather than a direct theoretical conflict.

## The Core Insight

The history of mind is not a sequence of competing answers,  
but a sequence of changing explanatory problems.

Different theories may appear to disagree,  
while in fact addressing different explananda.

This distinction provides a new way of interpreting both historical and contemporary debates.

## Extension to Artificial Intelligence

This pattern is visible in modern AI.

Different paradigms are often treated as competing solutions:

- Symbolic AI focuses on representation, structure, and abstraction  
- Embodied and enactive approaches focus on interaction, environment, and system dynamics  

However, these approaches may not be solving the same problem.

They may prioritize different explananda:

- performance  
- representation  
- understanding  
- interaction  

As a result, apparent conflicts in AI may reflect differences in explanatory priorities rather than direct contradictions.

## The Persistent Tension

The tension between abstraction and embodiment is not resolved.

It is reproduced across different theoretical frameworks:

- Plato → stability of knowledge  
- Aristotle → organization of living systems  
- AI → symbolic vs embodied cognition  

This suggests that modern AI inherits conceptual tensions rather than eliminating them.

## Implication for the Research Program

Phase I establishes a key principle for the Rethinking Mind project:

Understanding mind requires identifying  
what is being explained,  
not only how it is explained.

Future phases will examine how this tension evolves  
and whether it can be reformulated rather than resolved.
